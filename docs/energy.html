    Robotics for Energy.

    Where robots and autonomy are already at work across the energy system — from boiler walls and live transmission lines to solar fields, turbine blades, geothermal rigs, mines, data centers, and orbit. Curated, with a primary source on every entry.

    Bottom line

    The AI build-out has made energy the hottest robotics market nobody is tracking. Inspection robots are already standard at power plants and substations; autonomy is moving up the stack into building solar farms, repairing turbine blades, drilling geothermal wells, mining the critical minerals, and even assembling power in space. The same nexus driving the dataset — compute demand, reindustrialization, and physical AI — runs straight through the grid.

    Project Prime Mover

    Draft — for discussion

    Which energy tasks justify a robotics trial, what prevents a purchase, and which problems require federal action? This unofficial DOE inquiry asks operators and suppliers for evidence that can select projects for the draft Robotics Executive Order.

    Draft concept · Not an official DOE document

    Project Prime Mover: Robotics for Inspection, Production, and Laboratory Work

    Issuing office (concept)
    U.S. Department of Energy (lead) · Office of Science and Technology Policy (contributing)
    Status
    Unofficial draft for discussion
    Response window
    60 days from issuance
    Revision
    24 September 2026 · No official docket

    Proposed purpose: identify tasks where robotics could reduce hazardous exposure, increase accepted production, or shorten experimental turnaround. Ask respondents to document one operating task or failed deployment before recommending a program. The proposed DOE lead would use responses to select mission trials, refer rule-specific barriers to the responsible regulator, and identify research gaps. An RFI response is neither an award application nor a promise of purchase. No submission channel is open for this unofficial draft.

    Who should respond

    • Energy asset owners & operators
    • Robotics & autonomy developers
    • Manufacturers & EPC firms
    • Labor organizations
    • Universities & national laboratories
    • Investors
    • Standards bodies
    • State energy offices
    How to answer. Label responses with revision date, draft name, and question ID: 2026-09-24 · Full · B2. Answer only where you have evidence; suggest 500 words per question plus a table. IDs are scoped to the draft and revision. Mark statements as measured, estimated, or proposed. State unknowns and disclose vendor, operator, funder, or worker-representative interests.

    Start with one task and one site

    Attach a task record to any deployment claim. Use a site alias in a public response. Separate laboratory demonstrations, paid pilots, routine operations, and repeat orders; identify teleoperation and vendor support in each.

    • Work: asset type, task, operating envelope, customer role, dates, shifts, attempted tasks, and accepted results. Include rejected parts and aborted runs.
    • Baseline: current method, crew hours, inspection coverage or first-pass yield, outage hours, and cost per accepted unit. Compare the same workload and quality requirements.
    • Robot: hardware/software version, autonomous hours, remote and on-site interventions per 100 operating hours, downtime, recovery time, and safety events.
    • Economics: equipment, tooling, integration, qualification, training, service, consumables, and decommissioning costs; utilization assumption and the threshold for a repeat purchase.
    • Evidence: measurement method, sample size, uncertainty, independent verification, and release rights. Report measured exposure separately from claims about injuries avoided.
    Public response boundary. Do not include classified information, facility vulnerabilities, personal records, or confidential commercial data here. A real notice would need agency-approved handling instructions before requesting protected attachments. Public summaries can use ranges and redacted task records.

    Three missions to test first

    Editorial proposal, not a DOE selection. These missions offer a named buying decision and measurable work. Respondents may propose a different task with equivalent evidence.

    Inspect without sending a worker in

    Tank, pipe, and vessel inspection. DOE documents robotic tank inspection at Hanford. Ask an asset owner for required defect detection, coverage, retrieval method, exposure, and cost per accepted inspection. First test: seeded defects in a representative mock-up. No field trial without a recovery plan.

    Produce accepted energy-equipment parts

    Weld, finish, or tend machines for a defined part family. Start through MEP automation support. Ask the plant for drawing tolerances, changeover frequency, rework, integration hours, and cost per accepted part. Compare conventional automation with learned control; the buyer sets the acceptance test.

    Handle laboratory samples reliably

    Transfer and prepare samples under a lab's approved protocol. Ask for traceability errors, contamination, human interventions, and reproducible results per shift. Start with nonhazardous surrogates. Retain manual fallback; faster handling matters only if the experiment remains valid.

    What responses must let DOE decide

    Within 90 days after the response window closes, proposed: publish a task shortlist, an evidence-gap register, and referrals naming the responsible agency. For each shortlisted task identify a willing host, an acceptance authority, a funding/authority review, and the missing evidence before a trial. Publish reasons for deferring tasks. Missing reliability evidence goes to R&D; proven economics with a financing gap goes to lenders; a citation-specific barrier goes to its regulator.

    Agency roles: propose DOE Environmental Management for cleanup inspection and DOE Office of Science for laboratory tasks, subject to office agreement and mission authority. NIST/MEP would support manufacturing assessment; NIST would support measurement. OSTP would coordinate cross-agency referrals. DOE does not control private plants, mines, utility procurement, or other agencies' waivers.

    Questions

    Engagement interest — a checkbox block on the response form, not an essay question

    • Regional listening sessions
    • Technical workshops with universities / national labs
    • Hosting site visits or demonstrations
    • Contributing to shared testbeds
    • Joining an energy-robotics consortium
    • Piloting pooled-data governance

    Engagement tracks

    Beyond written responses, the inquiry proposes three tracks so the roadmap is built on observed evidence, not just filings:

    Regional listening sessions

    Operators and workers review the same task record. Output: disputed baseline assumptions, staffing effects, and conditions for a trial.

    Technical workshops

    Labs and independent evaluators turn proposed tasks into test protocols. Output: acceptance metrics, withheld test cases, data rights, and an estimated test cost.

    Site visits & demonstrations

    At consenting sites: inspect logs, observe a full work cycle including recovery, and compare claims with the task record. A demonstration alone cannot establish long-run reliability.
