The same nexus driving the dataset — compute demand, reindustrialization, and physical AI — runs straight through the grid.

    Project Prime Mover

    Draft — for discussion

    If Washington wanted to act on everything this page documents, step one is asking the right questions. This is a draft of that instrument — an unofficial concept for a national inquiry the Department of Energy could issue, with every question carrying an ID that's stable within its draft, so responses can be split, clustered, and synthesized straight into a report and roadmap.

    Draft concept · Not an official DOE document

    Project Prime Mover: National Inquiry on Robotics and Physical AI for the Energy and Manufacturing Base

    Issuing office (concept)
    U.S. Department of Energy
    Status
    Unofficial draft for discussion
    Response window
    60 days from issuance
    Docket (placeholder)
    DE-PM-2026

    The Department seeks input from industry, academia, labor, and the investment community to inform potential Departmental actions on robotics and physical AI across the energy and manufacturing sectors — where robots are already in service at power plants and substations and autonomy is moving up the stack into building solar farms, repairing turbine blades, drilling wells, mining critical minerals, and running materials-discovery labs. Responses will inform a public report and a technology-and-deployment roadmap covering applications, deployment barriers, workforce, supply chains, and research-and-development priorities. This inquiry would not commit the Department to any action and is not a funding solicitation.

    Who should respond

    • Energy asset owners & operators
    • Robotics & autonomy developers
    • Manufacturers & EPC firms
    • Labor organizations
    • Universities & national laboratories
    • Investors
    • Standards bodies
    • State energy offices
    Built to be processed. Answer only the questions where you have direct knowledge, and label every answer with the draft you answered and its question ID — e.g., Full · B2 — suggested limit 500 words per question, data as attachments. IDs are scoped to a draft: only one of these would ever be issued, and the same letter-number is reused across drafts for materially different questions, so the draft name is what keeps a tagged answer unambiguous. Each category feeds a named chapter of the planned roadmap (a few drafts route two related categories into one chapter), so tagged responses can be sorted, clustered by category, and synthesized without manual triage.

    Questions

    Engagement interest — a checkbox block on the response form, not an essay question

    • Regional listening sessions
    • Technical workshops with universities / national labs
    • Hosting site visits or demonstrations
    • Contributing to shared testbeds
    • Joining an energy-robotics consortium
    • Piloting pooled-data governance

    Engagement tracks

    Beyond written responses, the inquiry proposes three tracks so the roadmap is built on observed evidence, not just filings:

    Regional listening sessions

    Operators, vendors, and labor in energy-heavy regions — Gulf Coast, Appalachia, the Southwest solar belt, the Great Lakes manufacturing corridor.

    Technical workshops

    Co-hosted with universities and national laboratories on the hardest questions — shared testbeds (B5), R&D gaps (E1), and pooled training data (F1).

    Site visits & demonstrations

    At operating facilities — plants, solar builds, mines, labs — turning respondent claims into first-hand evidence for the roadmap.
