    Executive action and proposed bills

    The FCC’s July 28 Covered List addition rests on an interagency national-security determination, reproduced in Appendix C of its notice. It restricts new equipment authorizations, with conditional approvals; it is separate from Commerce’s September 2025 Section 232 investigation, which ended in the October 28, 2026 proclamation. The six legislative items above concern procurement, security review or coordination. They are proposals or House-passed text, rather than enacted robotics programs as of October 14, 2026.

    Policy options from this comparison

    These are our proposals, not recommendations attributed to the sources. Deployment trials for small manufacturers could measure uptime, cost per task and paid worker training; the risk is subsidizing purchases firms would make anyway. A published security approval route could preserve access to trusted equipment, but requires ongoing testing. A commission needs an implementation owner and milestones if its reports are to lead to decisions. Component support tied to customer orders could target shortages; broad subsidies may instead protect weak suppliers.

    Bottom line

    State and local rules shape robot operation and deployment. California regulates autonomous-vehicle testing and passenger service through separate permits. Pennsylvania permits qualifying delivery devices; Philadelphia is considering a delivery fee. Broad state tax incentives may also apply to robotics firms.
