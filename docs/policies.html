It restricts new equipment authorizations, with conditional approvals; it is separate from Commerce’s September 2025 Section 232 investigation, which ended in the October 28, 2026 proclamation.     Policy options from this comparison

    These are our proposals, not recommendations attributed to the sources. Deployment trials for small manufacturers could measure uptime, cost per task and paid worker training; the risk is subsidizing purchases firms would make anyway. A published security approval route could preserve access to trusted equipment, but requires ongoing testing. A commission needs an implementation owner and milestones if its reports are to lead to decisions. Component support tied to customer orders could target shortages; broad subsidies may instead protect weak suppliers.
