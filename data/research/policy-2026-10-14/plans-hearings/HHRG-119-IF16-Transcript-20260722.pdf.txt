 Good afternoon, and welcome to today's hearing.  We are 42
here to consider several bills that address important issues 43
facing our nation's communications ecosystem.  These range 44
from securing our networks from foreign threats and robocalls 45
to improving how we connect our rural constituents. 46
 We all know the frustration of receiving unwanted 47
robocalls.  Americans received an estimated 4.3 billion 48
robocalls last month alone.  Although some of these calls are 49
legal, calls that the recipient has consented to receiving 50
such as from a school or a pharmacy, illegal robocalls 51
continue to be a problem.  In fact, fraud perpetrated against 52
Americans by illegal robocalls costs an average of $22 53
billion annually, primarily affecting those who cannot afford 54
such losses. 55
 Additionally, fraudulent robocalls make us distrustful 56
of legitimate callers, causing us to ignore calls from 57
unknown numbers and often miss important calls.  Congress has 58
taken steps to address this problem.  In 2019 we enacted the 59
Pallone-Thune TRACED Act.  This law took important steps to 60
reduce illegal robocalls by requiring providers to implement 61
call authentication, increase fines for intentional 62
violations of the Telephone Consumer Protection Act, 63
authorize traceback efforts, and establish a Federal 64
