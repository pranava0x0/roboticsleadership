#### Application/sector ideas from the report (manufacturing-related)

The report breaks industrial robotics down by *application* — a lens we don't surface. (Energy is not a focus of this report, so nothing energy-specific to add from it; the energy angle is only the rare-earth/magnet component risk already captured above.)

- **Sector-application view: automotive vs electronics vs semiconductor vs textile (medium, manufacturing).** The report's most actionable application data is **China's foreign-reliance by sector**: China imports ~69% of its installed *automotive* robots (high path-accuracy, heavy-payload, technically demanding) but ~0% of its *textile* robots (auxiliary handling, low-demand) — and self-sources most electronics robots. Pair this with the **regional-optimization thesis**: robots get optimized for the dominant local industry, so Chinese robots skew toward electronics (high precision / low payload) while US demand skews automotive. A small per-sector breakdown (complexity, payload/precision profile, who leads, China import-reliance) would add a manufacturing-applications dimension to china.html or supply-chain.html that the current robot-class taxonomy (industrial/cobot/service/humanoid) doesn't capture.
- **Semiconductor wafer-handling robotics as a tracked niche (medium, manufacturing + semiconductor).** The report flags cleanroom-compatible, high-precision wafer-transfer robots as a distinct demanding application dominated by Japanese firms — RORZE (the last significant Japanese robotics startup, founded 1985), Yaskawa's SEMISTAR clean robots, Nidec. This sits at the intersection of our supply-chain "Compute & semiconductors" category and the industrial-arm incumbents; worth a short watchlist/cross-link since semiconductor-fab automation is strategically distinct from general factory arms. Add RORZE to `companies.json` if we want incumbent coverage of the wafer-handling segment.
- **Integration-services share as a deployment-cost fact (low, manufacturing).** The report's finding that **integration/system-integrator services are ~two-thirds of total robotics deployment cost** (design + installation + on-site maintenance) is a clean, citable data point that reframes "robot cost" debates — the arm is the minority of the spend. Good as a supply-chain fact or a themes.html note explaining why local production/service proximity drives deployment.

### Policy ideas to explore (added 2026-06-01 from a robotics news sweep)

- **State AMR & sidewalk-delivery-robot law tracker** — The operational rulebook for autonomous mobile robots is a fragmented state/municipal patchwork, and it's moving *now*: 20+ states already authorize sidewalk delivery robots (PA classifies them as "pedestrians"), while in May 2026 Philadelphia introduced a $1,000-per-delivery sidewalk surcharge and other cities push back. Our `policies.json` state coverage today is economic-incentive only (tax credits, jobs grants) — we track *who gets subsidized*, not *where a robot is legally allowed to roll*. Add a state-by-state matrix of speed caps (~10 mph), weight limits, pedestrian-classification, mandatory liability insurance, and operating-permit rules. Distinct enough from the incentives table to warrant its own surface or a `level: "Municipal"` tier. Sources: [Supply Chain Dive](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/delivery-robot-bills-laws-proliferate-state-legislatures/648303/), [Philadelphia Inquirer](https://www.inquirer.com/politics/philadelphia/uber-delivery-robots-center-city-regulation-20260518.html).
- **Robot liability & insurance gap theme** — The biggest *unfilled* policy hole for 2026: existing tort law assumes a human operator, so product-liability allocation for autonomous/humanoid systems is unsettled, and certified safety applications (ISO 10218:2025 / ANSI A3 R15.06-2025) are quietly becoming the de-facto bar insurers and courts use to judge "acceptable practice." Track as a theme + a watchlist of liability rulings, insurer requirements, and any federal/state move to codify allocation. Pairs with the compliance-checklist item above. Sources: [MLT Aikins](https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/connected-robots-connected-risk-robotics-liability-considerations/), [AI Frontiers](https://ai-frontiers.org/articles/the-robot-in-your-living-room-has-no-rulebook).
- **Workforce displacement, reskilling & "robot tax" policy tracker** — As humanoid unit costs fall toward $15–20K and shipments hit tens of thousands in 2026, the displacement-vs-augmentation debate is generating concrete policy proposals: robot-tax schemes, UBI pilots, lifelong-learning subsidies, and public-private reskilling partnerships. None are in our dataset yet despite being the politically hottest robotics topic. Add a theme + track specific bills/programs (federal and state workforce credits already in `policies.json` like NY Excelsior are the *supply* side; this is the *displacement* side). Sources: [Brookings — Robotization & occupational mobility](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/robotization-and-occupational-mobility/), [Economic Lens](https://economiclens.org/ai-and-automation-navigating-job-displacement-economic-inequality-in-2026/).
- **Humanoid-specific safety standards watch (ISO 25785-1, OSHA)** — Our compliance-checklist item covers *industrial* robot standards; humanoids need their own. ISO 25785-1 (in development) governs bipedal fall-risk via balance metrics and zone calculations — the first standard written for human-form factor. Separately, OSHA still has **no** robot-specific rule and leans on voluntary consensus standards + tort law; track whether/when a dedicated OSHA humanoid standard emerges. Sources: [There's a Robot for That — Humanoid Safety Standards 2026](https://www.theresarobotforthat.com/blog/humanoid-robot-safety-standards-2026/).
- **Global robotics-policy scoreboard (US vs China)** — We track US federal momentum and "China Manufacturing Scale" separately, but not the *policy* race head-to-head. China made embodied AI a 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030) priority, released the national Humanoid Robot & Embodied Intelligence Standard System (2026 edition) covering safety/ethics across the full lifecycle, stood up an MIIT Standardization Committee, and runs "Robot+"/"AI+Manufacturing" roadmaps targeting a doubling of robot density. A side-by-side scoreboard (national strategy, standards bodies, procurement preference, density targets) would sharpen the competitiveness story congressional-staffer users care about. Sources: [MERICS — Embodied AI](https://merics.org/en/report/embodied-ai-chinas-ambitious-path-transform-its-robotics-industry), [IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/china-makes-ai-powered-robots-core-of-national-strategy), [TechNode](https://technode.com/2026/02/28/china-releases-first-national-standard-framework-for-humanoid-robots-and-embodied-ai/).
- **Federal procurement / domestic-content (Buy-American) robotics thread** — The American Security Robotics Act (already tracked) is the leading edge of a broader pattern: barring "covered foreign entities" from federal UGV/humanoid procurement and pushing domestic-content preference. Track the downstream FAR rulemaking and any DoD/GSA acquisition guidance that operationalizes it — that's where the bill actually bites for vendors. Sources: [Global Policy Watch](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/04/the-next-frontier-of-supply-chain-security-congress-trains-its-sights-on-robotics/), [AUVSI](https://www.auvsi.org/news/auvsi-statement-on-introduction-of-the-american-security-robotics-act/).
